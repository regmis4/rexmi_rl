Selection: one leg motor SKU, two transmissions

Use RobStride RS06 for all 12 leg joints. Direct output for hip and thigh; 2:1 synchronous toothed-belt reduction for each knee. Total rotor-to-knee ratio is 18:1. This supersedes the earlier CubeMars design baseline.

Why RS06, not a larger but slower QDD?

RobStride's 13 July 2026 specification, pages 31–33, provides a usable loaded curve and overload tables. RS06 is 621 g, internal 9:1, nominal 48 V, specified operating range 15–60 V, 36 Nm peak. The 11 Nm rating is measured at 100 rpm with a 200 × 200 mm aluminum heatsink; it is not an enclosed-leg guarantee.

  • RS02 direct: 17 Nm peak is below the accepted leg cap. Additional gearing trades away the thigh speed and does not give the knee the same thermal margin.
  • RS03 / RS04 direct knee: nominal no-load speeds are only 195 / 200 rpm, close to the measured knee's 191.86 rpm. Loaded speed is lower; high peak torque alone does not solve this.
  • RS06: its loaded data include 20 Nm at 390 rpm and 36 Nm at 280 rpm. A 2:1 knee stage uses that speed capacity to lower motor torque.

Knee: select 2:1, not 3:1

N = ωmodule / ωknee = 48 / 24 = 2
τmodule ≈ τknee / (ηN), η = 0.95 assumed for forward motoring
Worst-run RMS: 15.80 / 1.90 = 8.31 Nm
Worst 10-second RMS: 22.08 / 1.90 = 11.62 Nm
At joint cap: 23.5 / 1.90 = 12.37 Nm
Maximum module speed: 20.09 × 2 × 60 / (2π) = 383.72 rpm

The conservative rectangle of 12.37 Nm at 383.72 rpm falls below the nominal loaded curve, which interpolates to about 20.90 Nm there. This pairs separate maxima deliberately as a conservative screen, unlike claiming they occurred together. At 90% stage efficiency the capped module demand is 13.06 Nm; speed is unchanged.

  • 2:1 lowers knee RMS enough to be useful while retaining nominal loaded-speed headroom. 2.25:1 already exceeds the last tabulated loaded-speed point at the maximum measured knee speed.
  • Thermal reality: continuous stall rating is only 8 Nm at module output; 11 Nm stall is listed for 200 s and 17 Nm for 15 s. Do not claim indefinite capped knee holding: 12.37 Nm exceeds continuous stall rating.
  • Vendor rotating overload table gives 17 Nm for 200 s, but it is not a repeated-cycle thermal model. The 11.62 Nm worst 10-second RMS and 1.36 s capped interval are plausible bench targets, not proof of 60-minute endurance.
  • Vendor low-speed equivalent inertia is 0.012 kg·m² at module output. Ideal extra 2:1 reflection gives 0.048 kg·m² at the knee before external pulleys. Verify the vendor definition and put this in the dynamics model; extra reduction is not free.

Belt transmission: the prototype choice

Use an enclosed synchronous toothed belt with an adjustable, locked tension setting. Start CAD with a 24-tooth driver and 48-tooth knee pulley, 5 mm pitch, 20 mm belt width as a packaging allowance—not a released belt specification. Select the final compatible tooth profile, cord construction, width and length using the manufacturer's load/life calculation.

Calculated geometry and forces

  • Pitch diameters: 38.20 mm driver; 76.39 mm driven.
  • At 23.5 Nm knee output, belt-span tension difference ΔF = τ/r = 615 N.
  • At 213 mm pulley center distance, approximate small-pulley wrap is 169.7°, about 11.3 teeth engaged; open-belt pitch length ≈607.7 mm.
  • These are design-geometry calculations, not a return to a hypothetical support lever arm. Actual pulley centers and belt path must come from SolidWorks. Do not alter the 213 mm leg geometry merely to fit a stock belt.

What tensioning does—and does not do

  • Preload prevents slack and tooth ratcheting; it does not eliminate tensile-cord stretch, tooth compliance or mount deflection.
  • Ideal equal-span no-slack lower bound: T₀ > ΔF/2 ≈308 N per span. Not an installation tension specification. Dynamic load, tooth profile, width and vendor guidance determine the real value.
  • Near-parallel-span shaft radial load is approximately Ttight + Tslack, not ΔF alone. Include pretension in bearing and housing loads.
  • Use independent supported pulley shafts if the actuator output cannot carry the belt overhung load. Set tension with a gauge, lock the adjuster and provide inspection access.

Use Gates' timing-belt theory and its tension calculator for the chosen belt family. A generic belt width or 'tight enough' setting is not a tooth-jump or fatigue calculation.

Proposed stiffness acceptance test

Initial engineering target: ≤0.5° elastic knee deflection at 23.5 Nm, measured across the external transmission, equivalent to kθ ≥2,693 Nm/rad. This is a proposed target, not a measured Unitree value. Measure reversal hysteresis separately; tighten the target if policy replay shows sensitivity.

Ideal two-span belt model: kθ ≈ 2(EA/Lspan) routput²
θtotal = θbelt + θteeth + θshaft + θbearings/housing
Required measured EA and tooth stiffness come from the selected belt, not assumed material modulus.

Add an encoder at the actual knee output: the RS06's output encoder is upstream of the external belt and cannot directly detect belt deflection or a skipped tooth. If the motor housing is mounted to the moving upper leg, qmodule = 2qknee + offset (with calibrated sign). A body-fixed housing needs a coupled coordinate mapping instead; do not blindly apply this equation.

What Unitree does, and when bevel gears make sense

Simplexity's first-hand Go2 teardown establishes integrated planetary joint actuators and shows the opened leg. The actuator's planetary reduction and any remote knee transmission are different stages. The iFixit teardown/CT is another primary reference. The exact Go2-W knee stage and tooth ratio are not verified here; do not label a Go2 patent or a motor-only teardown as a production Go2-W BOM.

A shaft/bevel system is not automatically stiffer. Verify θshaft = TL/(GJ), J = π(do⁴−di⁴)/32, then reflect its compliance through the gear ratio and add gear-mesh/bearing/housing compliance. Check bevel bending/contact fatigue, pinion tangential force 2T/dpitch, axial/radial thrust, alignment and lubrication. If adopted, preserve 2:1 total external reduction, not 2:1 at both bevel meshes. Gear module, face width and shaft diameter remain unreleased until geometry and fatigue loads are defined.

Wheels: the all-RobStride limit

Do not order wheel motors from this report. RS06 is the leg selection, not a justified wheel substitution. Even using its nominal 480 rpm no-load endpoint and 95% assumed external efficiency:

To retain 23.7 Nm peak: N ≥ 23.7 / (0.95 × 36) = 0.693
To reach 79.00 rad/s: N ≤ (480 × 2π/60) / 79.00 = 0.636
No overlap; loaded speed makes the conflict worse.

N <1 is a speed-increasing transmission. The 79 rad/s sample is braking, so this simplified motoring endpoint test alone is not a braking proof. However, RR also reaches 76.25 rad/s in motoring and FR drives at 21.02 Nm / 61.50 rad/s. A loaded, four-quadrant wheel design is still required. Choose either a non-RobStride faster wheel motor or explicitly test a reduced wheel envelope before releasing wheel hardware; neither change is made here.

Release checklist

  • Obtain exact RS06 revision, CAD model, available stock and quote. Manufacturer source dated 13 July 2026 takes precedence over older conflicting web snippets for this screen.
  • CAD: preserve joint axes and link lengths; include pulley bearings, tensioner, guard, actual-knee encoder and cooling path. Reconcile each link's mass, COM and inertia.
  • Bench: calibrated joint torque, repeated captured waveforms, low-speed holding, thermal soak, minimum battery voltage, braking/regen, belt reversal stiffness and tooth-jump tests.
  • Control: integrated RS06 drive/CAN integration, explicit joint-coordinate torque cap and speed envelope, watchdog and independent emergency stop. Do not apply the 23.5 Nm knee cap at the module output: with 2:1 gearing that would nearly double joint torque.
  • Battery/PCB: 48 V nominal curve is not a battery-pack selection. Keep actual bus inside allowed limits, including full charge and regen; size discharge protection from synchronized electrical measurements/model.
  • Simulation: add RS06 and transmission inertia, friction, backlash/compliance and real torque–speed/thermal limits to a separate configuration. Do not silently mutate the accepted baseline.
